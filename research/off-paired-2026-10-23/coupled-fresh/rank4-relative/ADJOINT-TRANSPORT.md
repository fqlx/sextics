# Exact horizontal transport of the scalar adjoint

This supplies actual horizontal correction currents for a change of transverse
coordinate. It does not solve the scalar forcing equation.

Keep the complete scalar closure in `SCALAR-HORIZONTAL-CLOSURE.md`. On the
finite coefficient-root cover `tau^3=H`, put

    b=C/tau^2, B=tau^2 D_C, T=D_H+(2C/(3H))D_C.

Then `[B,T]=0`, and the normalized operator is

    L_b*=B^4+B^3 c3(b)−B^2 c2(b)+B c1(b)−c0(b),

where the coefficient functions are the H=1 companion coefficients. Exactly,

    L*(tau^8 F)=L_b* F.

Thus normalized adjoint transport may be performed without losing the
horizontal current in the original scalar PDE.

## A bare-C transverse section with all Jacobians

Use `(a,C,z)` on the physical base, with

    H(a,C,z)=a^2+q^6/a^2+z^6+qz/2+1,
    q=3C−3az/2,
    H_a=P_a/a^2, h=partial_C H|a,z=P_C/a^2,
    delta=partial_C|a,z,
    r=1−2Ch/(3H), beta=tau^2/r,
    rho=H_a/tau^2=P_a/(a^2 tau^2).

The derivative delta also satisfies `delta(tau)=tau h/(3H)`. The identities

    delta=r D_C+h T,
    B=beta delta−beta h T

retain the transverse coefficient r. The volume `rho da dC dz` is precisely
`H^(-2/3) dH dC dz`. Both B and T have divergence zero for this volume.
For example, in the original physical `(x,y,z)` coordinates,

    H^(-2/3) J T=(b_y,−b_x,0),
    J=H_x C_y−H_y C_x.

This is an actual divergence-free Hamiltonian field, without replacing the
full scalar equation by a pure-component restriction.

For an arbitrary rational scalar F on this finite cover, define

    Dcal(F)=rho^(-1) delta(rho beta F).

The exact first-order transport identity is

    B(F)=Dcal(F)−T(beta h F).

Its last term is the explicit horizontal correction. Neither the inverse
Jacobian nor its derivative is dropped.

## Finite recurrence through all four derivatives

Set

    K_0(F)=0,
    K_(n+1)(F)=beta h Dcal^n(F)+B(K_n(F)).

Because B and T commute, induction gives

    B^n(F)=Dcal^n(F)−T(K_n(F)).

In particular put

    J(F)=K_4(F)+K_3(c3 F)−K_2(c2 F)+K_1(c1 F).

Then

    L_b*F=Lcal*F−T(J(F)),
    Lcal*F=Dcal^4F+Dcal^3(c3 F)−Dcal^2(c2 F)
             +Dcal(c1 F)−c0 F.

Consequently a proposed identity in the bare-C section,

    −Lcal*F+T(U_tilde)+D_z W=sigma,

has the actual original scalar current

    f=tau^8 F, U=U_tilde−J(F), W=W.

This formula gives the complete horizontal correction generated by the
physical Jacobian. It is finite and constructive for any supplied F. It
leaves the task of finding F,U_tilde,W, and any eventual finite descent,
fully explicit.

The normal primitive suggests the concrete starting expression

    R=−27C^3/a+(81/4)Cz^2a−(27/16)z^3a^2,
    g=R/C^3.

Its three bare-C exponents 0,−2,−3 annihilate the H^4 factor of the *bare*
Euler expression, but Dcal includes rho and beta. The recurrence above
specifies the missing drift terms; it does not claim they vanish or that
this starting expression solves the forcing equation.

`verify_adjoint_transport.py` checks the generic rational vector fields,
commutation, actual volume identities, and the first two transport stages.
The higher stages follow by the stated exact induction.

## Scalar-potential Jacobian convention

There is also a precise monic-operator version, avoiding any guess about a
fourth power of r. Let

    L_b=B^4−c3 B^3−c2 B^2−c1 B−c0,
    L_delta=beta^(-4) L_b(beta delta).

Here `L_b(beta delta)` means substitute `beta delta` for each B in the
forward operator, with every coefficient derivative retained. `L_delta` is
monic in delta. Let `L_delta*` be its ordinary formal delta-adjoint for volume
`da dC dz`. Then exactly

    Lcal*(G/(rho beta^4))=rho^(-1) L_delta*(G).

Indeed conjugating `Dcal` by multiplication by rho gives the operator
`delta beta`, and formal adjunction of the left factor `beta^(-4)` places
that factor on the right. Therefore the scalar current potential in the
original closure is

    f=tau^8 G/(rho beta^4)=tau^2 r^4 a^2 G/P_a,

and its horizontal correction is still `−J(G/(rho beta^4))`. The original
forcing becomes `rho sigma` in the monic bare-section equation. In a pure
one-dimensional coordinate change with `rho=1/beta`, this reduces to the
usual cubic law `F=beta^(-3)G` for the last cyclic current coefficient.
The transformed lower coefficients are essential; this convention does not
turn the bare polynomial-root observation into a solution.
