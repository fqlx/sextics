# An exact rational-curve seed in the nonlinear coordinates

No primitive or certificate is claimed. Let

\[
a=x^3,\quad q=xy=3C-3az/2,
\quad H=a^2+q^6/a^2+z^6+qz/2+1.
\]

On the endpoint surface, introduce

\[
r=(H+Cp+Z)/2,\qquad r+p^6/r=H+Cp.
\]

For any sixth root of unity \(\zeta\), impose
\(p=\zeta q\) and \(r=a^2\). The remaining equation is precisely

\[
q(z/2+\zeta C)+(z^6+1)=0.
\]

Thus at \(z^6+1=0\), \(C=-z/(2\zeta)\), this gives the explicit rational
curve

\[
p=\zeta(3C-3az/2),\qquad Z=a^2-q^6/a^2.
\]

The curve has parameter \(a\), and the equalities are rational identities.
This is a special-parameter seed, not a family of rational curves on a
generic fixed-\(C,z\) surface.

If \(C,z\) vary as well, the incidence locus is rational: writing
\(L=z/2+\zeta C\), one can solve

\[
q=-\frac{z^6+1}{L},\qquad
a=\frac{2C}{z}+\frac{2(z^6+1)}{3zL}.
\]

It has dimension two inside the fourfold. It does not dominate the
fourfold or provide a residue primitive automatically.

## A natural extra parameter

For \(p=sq\), \(r=s^6a^2\), the exact endpoint equation reduces to

\[
(s^6-1)a^2-z^6-1-q(z/2+sC)=0.
\]

This produces a three-dimensional incidence locus. On the physical
endpoint cover \(r=X_2^6\), \(p=X_2Y_2\), it has

\[
Y_2^6=p^6/r=q^6/a^2=Y_0^6.
\]

Consequently this deformation is the familiar union of hyperplane
sections \(Y_2=\xi Y_0\), \(\xi^6=1\). It is not yet a new dominant
curve family or a source-carrying correspondence. Any use of the seed in
an auxiliary Jacobian incidence construction must retain the physical
normal derivative and source coefficient.
